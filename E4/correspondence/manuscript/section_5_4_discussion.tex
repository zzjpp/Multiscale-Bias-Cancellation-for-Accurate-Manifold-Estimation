\paragraph{What ENF contributes beyond fourth-order accuracy.}
On the original finite-sample STSP curve experiment, ENF and continuous CP have almost identical final geometric errors: their median maximum errors are $0.022716$ and $0.022737$, respectively, and both have zero failed matches in 320 queries. CP has the smaller direct correspondence error in this example. The median total CPU costs are $2.276$ s for ENF and $25.562$ s for CP, including target representation and search. This implementation-specific comparison does not provide a universal complexity lower bound for CP. In the deterministic population scan, the fitted slopes of $E_{\mathrm{match}}$ are $4.0027$ and $4.0016$ for ENF and CP. Thus fourth-order correspondence accuracy is not exclusive to ENF. Its separate advantage is a directly computable local correspondence with controllable uniqueness and continuity. The oscillatory normal-graph example in the supplement isolates this distinction: global CP is nonunique and switches branches under tangential perturbations, whereas the local outward ENF intersection remains unique and continuous. This nonuniqueness does not contradict fourth-order position accuracy of the individual nearest points.
